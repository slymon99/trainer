# Block 1: re-entry — Aug 10 to Sep 7

**3 build weeks + 1 recovery/test week.** The block's spine is the **sweet spot ladder
at 90%** (see [`../plan.md`](../plan.md) → *The ladders*), with the Sunday durability
blocks running at tempo on their own track.

Three build weeks rather than four, deliberately: this block opens straight off a month
without structured training, and a 3-on/1-off rhythm is what the rest of the year runs
on. It also puts the test on recovered legs instead of at the end of a five-day trip.

**Why the ladder enters at rung 3 rather than rung 1.** This is not new ground. Between
January and May the same ladder was climbed to rung 4 — 2×20 three times (24 Jan,
11 Feb, 19 Feb), 3×15 twice (12 Mar, 30 Apr), then 2×25 on 14 May at HR 150–153, which
is 88–90% of LTHR and comfortable. Sixteen weeks for two rungs. Entering at rung 3 after
a month off structure is one deliberate step back from where that progression stalled.

**Thu 27 Aug goes to rung 9, not rung 6.** Rungs 6, 7 and 8 (4×15, 3×20, 2×30) are all
60 min in band, and by the ladder's own fragmentation logic all three are *easier* than
the 1×55 continuous ridden on 25 Aug. Taking them in order would put a softer session on
Thursday than Tuesday, which is what "never skip a rung" exists to prevent, not to cause.
1×60 is a real +5 min progression on Tuesday, it is a named rung rather than an invented
shape, and at 60 min in band it is about 10 TSS lighter than the 2×35 that was the other
candidate. `plan.md` rule 6 applies: the block ends at the test, not the last rung — and
this one now ends with the ladder finished.

**Ride it at 243 W and let it drift to 248 if the last 15 min are light. Don't chase
249.** Thursday's job from here is to arrive on 5 Sep able to produce a genuine max.

**Where the block goes somewhere new is week 3.** Nothing above 2×25 at 90% had ever been
ridden. Rung 5 (1×50, ridden as 1×55) is the first continuous effort past 32 minutes in
band since 14 Jan, and rungs 6–9 are all 60 min in band, where the only thing still
progressing is fragmentation. That is the part of the ladder the 2026 plateau never
reached, and it is the reason this block was worth running.

**The 25 Aug result is also the clearest evidence yet that 270 W is stale.** 249 W is 92%
of the working FTP and the session held 152 bpm — 89% of LTHR — flat for 55 minutes, and
he finished feeling good. The "90%" rungs are landing nearer 85–87% of what he can
actually do. `plan.md` is still right that the number doesn't move until the test, but it
means the last two rungs read harder on paper than they were, and the 5 Sep re-anchor
should move materially.

## The Sunday blocks are not rungs

They use the **tempo target, 85%**, and progress on their own slow track: 2×10 (Aug 16)
→ 2×12 (Aug 22, Aug 30). They're depleted, outdoors, and capped by the ride, so they
can't be compared like-for-like against an indoor rung and they don't advance the
ladder.

Their cost is measurable and it is the point: on 16 Aug, blocks at 82–88% after 3 h ran
HR **154–158**, against 141–146 for the same percentages fresh on 13 Aug. That +10–14 bpm
gap is fractional utilization being trained.

## Travel, Sat 22 – Sun 23 Aug

Visiting family, no trainer. **Sunday is a hike and off the bike entirely**, so all the
weekend's riding is Saturday. The hike and the family ride are both with parents in
their 70s — genuinely negligible intensity.

**Saturday is one ride of ~4h30**, logged as a single activity because the three parts
run back to back with only regroups between them:

| Part | | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:30 | Solo Z2, no intervals — ride the **top** of the band | 64–68% |
| 1:30–3:30 | Family ride, <140 W | 45–52% |
| 3:30–4:30 | 12 min steady, then **2 × 12 min durability blocks** | **85%** |

**The last hour is the session; the first three and a half hours are the depletion that
makes it worth riding.** ~975 kJ in Part 1 plus ~940 in Part 2 is about 1,495 kJ once
the family ride is discounted for being fat-dominant at 48% FTP — essentially exactly
the 1,500 kJ trigger. So the durability session isn't lost to the travel weekend; it
lands on Saturday instead of Sunday, and Aug 30 keeps its own.

That arithmetic is tight, which is why Part 1 is prescribed at the **top** of Z2 rather
than "easy". Letting it sag to 58% costs the session at hour four. NP still capped at
72%.

**Part 1 carries no intervals, deliberately.** Hill repeats on the local 3-min climb are
the obvious thing to put there and the wrong thing: 18–24 min at 90% in hour one is paid
for out of hour four, and hour four is the only place this weekend where depleted-legs
tempo is available. They would also score nothing — **the ladder counts indoor sessions
only**, and rungs 3 and 4 are ridden Tue 18 and Thu 20, which the travel doesn't touch.

**Twelve minutes of steady Z2 before the first block**, because Part 3 starts off a stop
and a block begun cold comes out ragged. If a long stop falls between the two blocks it
*is* the recovery — don't add 5 min of spinning on top.

**Fuel against riding time, not elapsed.** 4h30 of riding at 80–90 g/h, and with
regroups this is a six-hour day on the clock. Depletion carries across the stops.

**Friday 21 takes the backfill: 2 h easy indoors instead of 60 min.** Sunday off the
bike leaves the week short and this is the cheapest place to find it. It sits directly
in front of Saturday, so it is capped hard at NP ≤72% and cut to 90 min if the legs are
heavy — hour four on Saturday is the priority.

**Assumed:** Friday 21 is still at home with the trainer. If the drive is Friday
morning, drop the 2 h rather than trying to relocate it — there is nowhere free left to
put it.

### Sat 5 Sep: the test

Strava segment **16244804**, "Wachusett Mtn from 140 Climb" — 8,362 m, 370 m of gain,
4.4% average, a little rolling. It begins **33.6 miles into the ride**, unavoidably
~2h20 in, because he is riding to the climb rather than driving to it. That has been
considered and declined; it is a fixed constraint, not an open optimisation.

**The test and the leaderboard attempt are the same effort, and the instruction is
max.** **Target 285 W, band 278–293** — leaderboard-derived watts, not a fraction of FTP,
the same exception the Wednesday sprints get. That is worth 21.5–22.8 min depending on
which end of the calibration holds; a top-10 time near 23 min costs 261–282 W, so the
target clears it with margin and **300 W is not required**. Full derivation, the
watts-to-time table, the pacing shape and why bicarbonate is not a lever are in
[`decisions.md`](decisions.md) → *Wachusett*. Read the 10th-place **time** off the board
and convert it there; don't model rivals' watts. Ride *toward* the number; don't pace
for it.

**A ~24-min max is a better eFTP input than a 20-min** — it sits closer to 60-min
power. So Wachusett is the block test, not a rehearsal for one.

**The approach inverts the Aug 22 rule.** There, the top of Z2 was prescribed to reach
depletion, because depletion was the point. Here depletion is the enemy: bottom of Z2,
NP ≤65%, soft-pedal every riser, small ring. The road is rolling and it will fight you —
in 2025 he rode this approach at NP 182 (67% FTP) without trying.

**90 g/h from minute 20**, which he has trained. At ~160 W for 2h20 he oxidises roughly
63 g/h of carbohydrate, so absorbing 70–80 leaves endogenous glycogen use near zero and
he should arrive essentially glycogen-neutral. The residual handicap is fluid loss and
thermal strain — call it **2–4%**, not the 5–8% the durability literature quotes for
genuinely depleted efforts. Start early for the coolest dew point available.

**Stop at ~33 mi, before the segment starts.** In 2025 he stopped *inside* it and lost
421 s of elapsed time; leaderboards score elapsed, so that stop cost more than every
equipment choice combined. Stopping just short of the start is free. There: drop tools,
pump and the spare bottle (~1.5 kg, ~17 s), top up, take a gel, roll to the start and go.

The levers all get bought at that stop — Crr .005 tyres/pressure ~19 s, −1.5 kg ~17 s,
drops not hoods ~15 s, skinsuit ~7 s. **About 59 s all in, which is more than the 2–4%
the approach costs.** Worth saying plainly: the optimisations more than cover the price
of riding there.

**Saturday is a five-hour, ~230 TSS day inside a recovery week. That is accepted, not an
oversight** — the week has one big day in it and the rest of the week is trimmed to make
room. **Tue 1 Sep drops to the light gym template** for the same reason: he is only
three sessions into loaded legs, still inside the window where unaccustomed eccentric
work costs the most, and four days out from the biggest single day of the plan is not
where to pay that. The one-session-a-week floor is met by it, and loaded legs resume the
following Tuesday.

### Sun 6 Sep: re-anchoring

Take intervals.icu's eFTP off the climb and follow the checklist in
[`../plan.md`](../plan.md). It has to be set before the next block's first session,
because every percentage from then on resolves against it.

**If it comes in at or below 270: do not lower it.** The ride was warm, rolling and
2h20 deep, which is exactly the exemption `plan.md` names. Retest indoors within 10 days
instead.

**Open, and worth deciding on the day:** whether to run a controlled indoor 20-min in
the following week *unconditionally* rather than only as a contingency. Cost is one
session; the gain is a reference directly comparable to January and December, and the
gap between it and the Wachusett number is a direct read on durability — the adaptation
this plan is chasing and currently has no instrument for.

## Load

Weekly TSS ran 352 → 382 → 483 across weeks 1–3 of the original shape. CTL was 52.5 on
18 Aug against a pre-illness peak of 65.1 (6 Jul) and an all-time peak of 68.8
(28 Mar), so this is still re-entry rather than novel
territory by CTL, though weekly TSS passed June's best in week 1.

**TSB was −17.6 on 18 Aug and the cost of this block is duration, not peak.** The two
hardest sessions in it land in week 3; nothing further gets added to week 3 without
something coming out. Rung 9 (1×60 @ 90%) is 60 min in band, the same as the rung 6 it
replaces and about 20 TSS lighter than the session that originally held the slot
(3×20+15 @ 85–88%, 75 min in band). That is why it is the right substitution and 2×35 @
90% — 70 min in band, and not on the ladder at all — is not.

**Markers on 16 Aug:** HRV 7d dipped to 72.1 (−8.0 vs the ~80 baseline) on 13 Aug and
recovered to 79.0; RHR 7d drifted 42.6 → 45.0 over nine days. One marker moving, not
two, so no back-off is triggered. **The check-in is the input the markers can't
supply** — both logged answers were positive, and a fading reason to train is the one
thing none of the above would detect.
