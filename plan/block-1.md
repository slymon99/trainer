# Block 1: re-entry — Aug 10 to Sep 13

**4 build weeks + 1 recovery/test week.** Sub-threshold and sweet spot, with the
Sunday durability session introduced. Ends with the test that re-anchors FTP before
the main threshold block.

Heat is at its worst right now, which is exactly why this is the block that leans on
indoor aerobic load. CTL target is in [`roadmap.md`](roadmap.md).

| Week | Dates | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aug 10–16 | Sunday blocks 2×10 |
| 2 | Aug 17–23 | Tuesday 3×13. **Sat–Sun away from home** — Saturday carries the 2×12 durability blocks (see below) |
| 3 | Aug 24–30 | Tuesday 3×15 — hits the sub-threshold TiZ target. Thursday 3×20 + 15 completes the sweet spot progression. Sunday 2.5 h + 2×12 |
| 4 | Aug 31 – Sep 6 | Tue–Wed easy, then the **Massachusetts camp, Thu 3 – Mon 7**. Thursday carries the block's last sub-threshold; Saturday is the 5 h with the climb. **Shape decided Sun 30 Aug** |
| 5 | Sep 7–13 | Camp's last day Mon 7, then recovery + **20-min test Sat 12 Sep** |

## Is the ramp sustainable? (checked 16 Aug, against actuals)

Actual weekly load 352 → 382 → **483**; planned from here 525 → 541 → 521 → 352.
Judge it on build weeks only — averaging the recovery week in flatters it.

**"Regaining June territory, not novel load" is now only half true, and the halves
disagree.** By weekly TSS it has expired: June's best week was 469 and week 1 passed
it, so weeks 2–4 are novel. By CTL it holds — June peaked at **CTL 62.1** (5 Jul)
against a projected 62.1 at the end of week 4 and **59.9 at the test**, level with the
June peak and a shade over the 58.6 the block was designed around. CTL is the better
measure of the two here, so the ramp stands.

**The cost is duration, not peak: TSB sits at ≈ −20 for two consecutive weeks**, then
−25.6 at the end of the camp. That is the thing to watch, and it is why nothing
further gets added to weeks 2–4 without something coming out.

Markers on 16 Aug: HRV 7d dipped to 72.1 (−8.0 vs the ~80 baseline) on 13 Aug and
**recovered to 79.0** (−1.1); RHR 7d **drifted 42.6 → 45.0** over nine days. One
marker moving, not two, so no back-off is triggered. **The missing input is the
check-in** — neither logged answer was asked cold, and that is the signal none of the
above can detect.

## Travel, Sat 22 – Sun 23 Aug

Visiting family, no trainer. **Sunday is a hike and off the bike entirely**, so all
the weekend's riding is Saturday. The hike and the family ride are both with parents
in their 70s — genuinely negligible intensity, so Sunday is a rest day that happens
to involve walking, and the family ride is recovery, not volume with a social element.

**Saturday is one ride of ~4h30**, logged as a single activity because the three
parts run back to back with only regroups between them:

| Part | | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:30 | Solo Z2, no intervals — ride the **top** of the band | 64–68% |
| 1:30–3:30 | Family ride, <140 W | 45–52% |
| 3:30–4:30 | 12 min steady, then **2 × 12 min durability blocks** | 85–88% |

**The last hour is the session; the first three and a half hours are the depletion
that makes it worth riding.** ~975 kJ in Part 1 plus ~940 in Part 2 puts you around
1,900 kJ raw at 3:30, or **~1,495 once the family ride is discounted for being
fat-dominant at 48% FTP** — essentially exactly the 1,500 kJ trigger. So the
durability session isn't lost to the travel weekend at all; it lands on Saturday
instead of Sunday. **Aug 30 keeps its own 2×12**, which makes this an *extra*
durability session in the block rather than a relocated one.

That arithmetic is tight, which is why Part 1 is prescribed at the **top** of Z2
rather than "easy". Letting it sag to 58% costs the session at hour four. NP still
capped at 72% — purposeful, not hard.

**Twelve minutes of steady Z2 before the first block.** Part 3 starts off a stop, and
the plan's rule applies: a block begun cold comes out ragged. If a long stop falls
between the two blocks it *is* the recovery — don't add 5 min of spinning on top.

**Part 1 carries no intervals, deliberately.** Hill repeats on the local 3-min climb
are the obvious thing to put there and they are the wrong thing: 18–24 min at 89–93%
in hour one is paid for out of hour four, and hour four is the only place this
weekend where depleted-legs sweet spot is available. They would also score nothing —
**TiZ is counted indoors only**, and both of the block's TiZ targets are met on Tue 25
and Thu 27, which the travel doesn't touch. Riding up the climb in Z2 is fine; doing
repeats on it is not.

**Fuel against riding time, not elapsed.** 4h30 of riding at 80–90 g/h, and with
regroups this is a six-hour day on the clock. Depletion carries across the stops —
muscle glycogen barely resynthesises in 20 min — so the parts stack even though the
ride doesn't feel continuous. Arriving at Part 3 under-fuelled just means riding it
badly.

**The practical catch is company, not physiology.** Two 12-min blocks mean riding
ahead twice on what is otherwise a social hour. Agree the regroups before starting,
or pick a stretch you can loop back on.

**Friday 21 takes the backfill: 2 h easy indoors instead of 60 min.** Sunday off the
bike leaves the week ~60 TSS short and this is the cheapest place to find it; the
load is front-loaded into a week that ends with a hike and nothing else.

**It sits directly in front of Saturday, and that is what this costs.** There is no
rest day between Friday's volume and Saturday's blocks. So the ride is capped hard at
NP ≤72% and cut to 90 min if the legs are heavy — hour four on Saturday is the
priority, and turning up flat to protect half an hour of Friday Z2 is a bad trade.
Week 2 lands at 525 TSS against 529 as originally planned.

**Mon 24 is a 45-min recovery spin, not a day off.** Sunday's hike is a lot of
eccentric loading on the descents, and easy blood flow the day after beats a second
consecutive zero-bike day. It is capped at <60% and deliberately tiny: week 3 is the
week both TiZ progressions complete, Tue 25 and Thu 27 are the sessions the block has
been building toward, and the week is already at its planned load without help from
Monday. On a genuinely wrecked morning, take the day off instead. Week 3 lands at
541 TSS against 514 planned — the overshoot is Monday's 23 and it is all recovery
pace.

**Assumed:** Friday 21 is still at home with the trainer, travelling Friday evening
or Saturday morning. If the drive is Friday morning, drop the 2 h and take the week
at ~460 TSS rather than trying to relocate it — there is nowhere free left to put it.

## The Labor Day camp, Thu 3 – Mon 7 Sep

A cycling trip in Massachusetts. **Five riding days**, Thursday and Monday being
travel days at either end. Thursday has a trainer; Friday is a workday ridden
outdoors; Sunday is free; Monday is a ride before the drive home.

A recovery week and a riding trip cannot be the same week, so **recovery comes after
the camp** and the test is Sat 12 Sep. It costs a week against the roadmap, which
Block 5's buffer absorbs; reclaim it by running Block 3 as 2 build + 1 if you're
ahead at that point.

The camp is **a load block, not an interruption** — consecutive long aerobic days
are the limiter's preferred stimulus, and the riding-with-friends part is a sub-goal
in its own right.

| Day | Ride | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Thu 3 | 67 min indoors, travel day | **2 × 15 @ 90–93%** — the block's last sub-threshold |
| Fri 4 | 2 h easy outdoors, around work | none |
| Sat 5 | **5 h, the key ride** | **the 1,100 ft climb @ 85–88%**, one sustained effort |
| Sun 6 | 2.5 h | 2 × 15 @ 82–85% after the first hour |
| Mon 7 | 2 h, then drive home | none |

**Saturday is the best version of the durability session in the whole block.** The
climb sits at roughly the halfway point, which puts you at **~1,575 kJ** when you
reach it — past the 1,500 trigger, so it's a genuine depleted-legs effort. 1,100 ft
at sweet spot is **~21–25 min** depending on gradient (unmeasured — ride to the top,
don't stop at a clock time). That's about the same in-band time as the 2×12 you've
been building, as one continuous block, on a steady gradient where the band actually
holds, deeper into the ride than you can normally get. Ride the whole climb as one
effort; don't chop it into reps.

**The last sub-threshold session goes on Thursday, not Tuesday.** It's the only
remaining trainer day, a three-point band doesn't hold on open road, and **TiZ is
counted indoors only**. It also closes the gap in the block's primary stimulus to
9 days (25 Aug → 3 Sep) rather than the 17-day hole to the test. Tue 1 goes easy and
Wed 2 is off, so a five-day camp starts fresh rather than three days deep. If the
travel day eats Thursday, **drop it** — don't move it to Friday, which has to stay
easy in front of Saturday.

**Monday now sits inside the recovery week**, which is the one structural cost of
the camp growing to five days. It is pure Z2 with nothing in it for that reason, and
Tue 8 – Fri 11 stay genuinely easy: only five days separate the last camp ride from
the test.

**Costed:** week 4 lands at **521 TSS** (deepest TSB −25.6, no deeper than the plan
already accepts), and the test comes at **CTL 59.9, TSB −2.2** — a point of CTL above
the three-day version for an essentially unchanged hole. The greedier shapes were
modelled and rejected: keeping Tuesday's quality *and* the full camp reaches CTL 62.4
but puts week 4 at 655 TSS, 20% above the heaviest week on record, at TSB −35.

**Camp shape.** Flat and rolling outside the one climb removes the descents that
normally hand you free recovery, so NP creep is the risk — the easy-ride NP cap
matters *more* here, applied outside the blocks. Early-September dew points in
Massachusetts usually run 60–68°F, so expect the heat factor to pull the climb down a
notch; check the morning of. Fuel to the camp-day numbers against *riding* time —
five consecutive days is where under-fuelling compounds.

**Sunday is the droppable one.** If Saturday goes long or hard, ride 2 × 12 instead
of 2 × 15 or make it pure Z2. Nothing about the camp is worth arriving at the test
having overcooked a Sunday.

## Week 4's shape is decided Sun 30 Aug, not now

Weeks 2 and 3 are identical under every variant, so this waits for three more weeks
of data. Pre-committed, to be applied cold:

| Signals on Sun 30 Aug | Week 4 |
|---|---|
| Check-in green, HRV and RHR steady, week 3 completed at target | **Thu 3 Sep sub-threshold 2×15, then all five camp days** |
| Mixed — one marker drifting | **Drop Thursday's quality. All five camp days, Sat's climb at 83–85%** |
| Check-in red, either hard stop, or clearly overreached | **Ride Fri–Sun only; Sunday pure Z2, skip Monday** |

**Why a sub-threshold session is on the table at all.** Without one, the last is
Tue 25 Aug and the next quality effort is the test on 12 Sep — a 17-day gap in the
block's primary stimulus. Thursday 3 Sep cuts that to 9 days without denting the camp,
because it lands two days before Saturday and Friday is easy anyway.

**Known cost:** the end of the camp is the deepest fatigue of the plan so far
(TSB −25.6). That is acceptable with a recovery week behind it — it would not be
acceptable arriving at a test, which is precisely why the test is 12 Sep. It is only
safe if Tue–Wed is genuinely easy, Friday and Monday are genuinely Z2, and Sunday
gets cut when Saturday was big. **The whole camp is built around one ride**; the other
four days are there to not compromise it and to not compromise the test behind it.
