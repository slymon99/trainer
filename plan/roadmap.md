# Block roadmap and test schedule

Targets are `%FTP` throughout, against whatever is in intervals.icu sport settings
at the time. Each block's test re-anchors that number, so the same percentage means
more watts in each successive block — that *is* the progression.

| Block | Dates | Structure | CTL target | Content and rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Re-entry** | Aug 10 – Sep 13 | 4 build + 1 | 48 → **58–60** | Sub-threshold and sweet spot; Sunday durability introduced. Can't build on CTL 48, and heat is at its worst now, so this is the block where accumulating aerobic load indoors costs least. Ends with the re-anchor that fixes the core error. Detail in [`block-1.md`](block-1.md). |
| **2. Threshold / muscular endurance** | Sep 14 – Oct 18 | 4 build + 1 | **65–70** | **The main event.** Long threshold: 2×20 → 2×25 → 3×20 → 2×25 at 95–100%. Sunday durability blocks grow to 3×15 at 83–87% after 3 h. This is the limiter, so it gets the most weeks and the freshest legs. Weather turns cool mid-block — attribute part of the power bump to conditions. |
| **3. Over-unders + ceiling check** | Oct 19 – Nov 15 | 3 build + 1 | **68–72** | Over-unders, e.g. 3×12 min alternating 2 min @ 105% / 2 min @ 92%. **One VO2 session per 10–14 days, maintenance only.** After five weeks of steady-state threshold, lactate-shuttling work returns more than more of the same. |
| **4. Peak threshold** | Nov 16 – Dec 13 | 3 build + 1 | **70–75** | Longest intervals of the year: 3×20, 2×25 at 98–103%. Durability in the cold. Same content as Block 2 at higher watts. Cool weather means the year's best absolute power — this is where 288 gets built. |
| **5. Sharpen, test, planned transition** | Dec 14 – Jan 3 | 2 sharpen + 1 test/taper | — | The 288 attempt on Alpe du Zwift, then **2 weeks deliberately unstructured.** The buffer absorbs one missed test or one bad cold. The planned transition directly addresses what happened after June: the next disengagement is on the calendar, so it can't ambush you. |

Reps top out at 25 minutes deliberately. FTP is roughly 60-minute power, so 2×30 at
or above 100% isn't a session — it's an FTP test with a rest stop, and it would get
read as training.

## Test schedule

| When | Test | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Sat 12 Sep** (end Block 1) | 20-min max, indoor, cool, after 2 easy days | Cheap, low recovery cost, re-anchors before the main threshold block |
| **Late Oct** (end Block 2) | **Alpe du Zwift**, same protocol as January, plus a **4-min max** on a separate day | Alpe is the best anchor — directly comparable to January. The 4-min shows whether the fractional-utilization ratio is moving |
| **Late Nov** (end Block 3) | 20-min max | Cheap check before the final push |
| **Late Dec / early Jan** (end Block 5) | **Alpe du Zwift** — the 288 W attempt | Same protocol, same setup, cool. At 288 W it takes ~47–48 min, still solidly FTP territory |

Alpe du Zwift as the recurring anchor is a genuine asset: a repeatable ~50-min max
effort in controlled conditions with a January reference. Same fans, same fuelling,
same time of day, every time.

Take intervals.icu's eFTP off the test rather than recomputing by hand, and follow
the re-anchoring steps in [`../plan.md`](../plan.md).

**If the October 4-min max has dropped below ~350 W** while FTP is climbing, the VO2
dose has been too low — add a VO2 session per fortnight in Block 4. If it held near
365 W, the plan is working as diagnosed.
