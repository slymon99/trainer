# Strength training

Two lifts a week, stacked onto the days that are already hard. This file holds the
protocol and the progression, because it runs on a different clock to the bike — it
spans every block rather than resetting at each one.

## What this is for, and what it isn't

**It will not move the December number.** Strength work in trained cyclists shows up
as improved efficiency and better-held power late in long efforts, on an 8–12 week
timescale — not as an FTP jump. It does not touch the diagnosed limiter, which is
mitochondrial density and lactate clearance at threshold. Expect ~0 W on the 12 Sep
test and ~0 W in late October. A small durability benefit by Blocks 3–4 is plausible
and is the most that should be claimed.

**The reasons it's in the plan anyway:**

- **Bone density.** Cycling is non-impact, and road cyclists measurably lose lumbar
  and femoral BMD against sedentary controls — high volume, no skeletal loading, and
  calcium lost in sweat. Nothing else in 10–11 h/week of riding loads the skeleton.
  Twice-weekly axial loading is the countermeasure.
- **Crash and injury resilience**, and connective tissue that pedalling never loads.
- **A base laid at 27 rather than at 35.** This compounds; starting late doesn't.

None of that appears in a power number, which is why grading it on watts was the
wrong frame. It's a health intervention with a small performance side effect, and it
is scheduled accordingly — subordinate to the bike, always.

## Where it sits in the week

**Lift after the bike, on the days that are already hard.** Ideally ≥3 h later
— ride at lunch, lift in the evening. Back-to-back is an acceptable fallback and
still beats moving the lift to an easy day; it just costs some lifting quality.

| | Variant A | Variant B (hard group ride Sunday) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tue** | **Lift A — heavy.** After the sub-threshold session | **Lift A — heavy.** Unchanged |
| **Thu** | **Lift B — light.** After the sweet spot session | **None.** Thursday's structure is already dropped |

**Tuesday's lift is the anchor and never moves. Thursday's is droppable and goes
whenever Thursday's bike session goes** — the same architecture as the bike, for the
same reason. A Variant B week already carries a hard group ride; adding a second lift
to it puts peak soreness on the Sunday.

**Why not Monday, Friday or the weekend:**

- **Monday** is the only genuine rest day, and rest days are the answer to the
  burnout risk that ended the last block. It also follows a 3–3.5 h Sunday, which is
  the worst possible state to lift heavy in.
- **Friday** puts peak DOMS — 24–48 h — directly onto Sunday's durability session,
  the one session training the exact missing adaptation. Non-starter.
- **Wednesday** is the recovery bridge between the two hard days, and carries the
  sprints.

Stacking both lifts onto Tuesday and Thursday keeps every easy day genuinely easy and
leaves ~68 h between the last lift and Sunday. Cost is ~1 h a week on top of the
~10.5 h of riding, on days that are already committed.

**Bike first, lift second, always.** Prior heavy lifting degrades subsequent cycling
quality more than the reverse, and bike quality is what's being protected.

## Phases

| Phase | When | Session | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Re-entry** | Tue 18 – Thu 27 Aug (4 sessions) | 3 × 8, **RPE 5**, 4+ reps in reserve | Movement pattern and connective tissue. Deliberately sub-stimulus — this phase exists to pay the novelty tax cheaply |
| **Bridge** | Tue 1 Sep only | Same as re-entry | Trip week. **Nothing Fri–Sun on the trip** |
| **Test week** | Tue 8 Sep only | 2 sets per exercise, re-entry load | **Nothing after Wed 9 Sep.** The test is protected |
| **Strength** | Block 2, Tue 15 Sep – Thu 15 Oct | 3–4 × 5–6, **RPE 7–8** (2–3 RIR) | Where load actually starts moving. Movements are no longer novel, so DOMS is minimal |
| **Maximal** | Blocks 3–4, Oct 19 – Dec 13 | 3–4 × 3–4, **RPE 8**, 3 min rests | The cycling-relevant end. **Volume comes down as load goes up** — CTL is at its year high here |
| **Maintenance** | Block 5, Dec 14 – Jan 3 | 1×/week, 2 × 4, same load | One session a week maintains maximal strength for 10+ weeks. **Nothing in the 5 days before the Alpe attempt** |
| **Off-season** | Jan – Feb | Lifting can lead | The one window where it's allowed to cost bike quality |

**Starting now rather than after the block test** is deliberate. The re-entry phase
has to be light whatever the calendar looks like, and Block 1's sub-threshold and
sweet spot work tolerates mild soreness far better than Block 2's 95–100% threshold
reps will. Paying the novelty tax against the block that can absorb it is worth more
than protecting two build weeks of a re-entry block.

**Every recovery week, at every block:** one session, Tuesday, two sets per exercise
at the usual load. Never introduce a movement or a load jump in a recovery week, and
never lift in the 5 days before a test.

## The session

Full gym. ~35 min heavy, ~25 min light. Six movements, and that's the ceiling — this
is not a lifting programme with a lifting goal.

| | Heavy (Tue) | Light (Thu) |
|---|---|---|
| Back squat | 4 × 5 | 3 × 5 @ 90% of Tuesday's load |
| Romanian deadlift | 3 × 6 | 2 × 6 |
| Bulgarian split squat | 2 × 6 / leg | — |
| Standing calf raise | 3 × 8 | 2 × 8 |
| Barbell row | 2 × 8 | — |
| Dead bug or ab wheel | 2 sets | 2 sets |

Rep counts above are the Block 2 bracket; the phase table sets the bracket, the
progression rule below moves the load inside it. From Block 3 the split squat and row
drop out — volume is what gets cut when the bike load peaks, not intensity.

**Calf raise earns its place**: ankle stiffness is where force gets lost between hip
and pedal, and it was in the original cyclist protocols for that reason. **Squat and
RDL earn theirs twice over** — they're the two movements actually loading the spine
and hip, which is the bone-density argument.

**Never train to failure.** Failure is what generates the soreness that wrecks
Wednesday and Sunday, and it buys nothing here. The calibration for the first
session: if you notice it on Wednesday's bike, it was too hard.

## Progressing the load

Double progression. **The rep bracket is fixed by the phase; the load moves inside
it** — the same shape as the bike's rule, where duration moves inside a block and
watts move between them.

- When **every working set** hits the top of the bracket with 2 reps still in
  reserve, add load next session: **+5 kg** squat and RDL, **+2.5 kg** everything
  else.
- **Never add load and sets in the same session.**
- If a top set drops below the bracket, hold the load — don't chase it. Two
  consecutive misses at the same load means back off 10% and rebuild.
- Expect fast progress for 6–8 weeks — that's neural, and it's what coming back from
  rusty looks like — then a slow grind. The grind is the signal to drop the rep
  bracket, not to add sets.

**The ceiling is a back squat around 1.3–1.5× bodyweight for a set of 5** — roughly
95–108 kg at 72 kg. Past that, further strength returns nothing to cycling. Stop
progressing and hold.

## Backing off

Three objective readouts, all already collected, all free:

| Signal | Read on | What a decline means |
|---|---|---|
| **Peak sprint watts** vs the 917 / 806 W anchor | Wednesday's 4 × 15 s | The lift is too heavy, or too close to the bike |
| **Rep 3, last 5 min** — can it hold 90–93% with HR flat | Tuesday | The lift is eating the anchor session |
| **The final 2 × 10 @ 85–88%** | Sunday | Cut lifting immediately — this is the session the block is built around |

Plus the plan's existing markers: HRV and RHR two-of-three, and the weekly check-in
question.

**The ladder, in order:**

1. Any readout down two weeks running → **drop Thursday's lift.** Tuesday only.
2. Still down → **no lifting until after the next block test.**
3. **Bike quality wins, every time.** There is no version of this where the lift is
   protected at the expense of Tuesday or Sunday.

## Fuelling and mass

**Protein 1.6–1.8 g/kg/day — 115–130 g at 72 kg**, with 25–40 g in the hour after
lifting. This is almost certainly more than currently eaten and it's the one
nutritional change that matters; the existing no-deficit-during-build rule covers the
rest. If the lift is separated from the bike by 3 h, eat carbohydrate in between —
lifting on the glycogen left over from a sub-threshold session is how a session gets
wasted.

**Mass should barely move.** Heavy, low-volume, twice-weekly work at eucaloric intake
alongside 10–11 h/week of riding adds close to nothing — expect 0–1 kg, mostly
glycogen and water in the first fortnight. If it settles more than ~1.5 kg up, note
that the goal is **288 W**, not 4.0 W/kg: at 73.5 kg, 288 W is 3.92 W/kg. The watts
target stands; the W/kg line in the goal is descriptive.

## Assumptions

1. **Full gym with barbell and rack; prior lifting experience, currently rusty.**
   If squat technique isn't actually solid under load, the re-entry phase runs 4
   weeks instead of 2 and Block 2 starts lighter. A coached session or two is cheap
   insurance against the one injury that would cost more than the whole plan.
2. **No back or knee history given.** If there is one, trap-bar deadlift replaces the
   RDL and squat depth gets capped above the painful range.
3. **The bike session and the lift land on the same day with a gap.** If the only
   option is back-to-back, that's still the right call — just expect lower lifting
   quality, and progress load more slowly.
4. **No BMD measurement exists for this athlete.** The bone argument is population
   data on road cyclists, not a scan. A DEXA would settle it and isn't needed to
   justify twice-weekly loading.
