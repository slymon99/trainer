# Training plan — August to December 2026

**Goal:** 288 W FTP (4.0 W/kg at 72 kg) by the end of December. No target event.
Durable fitness to build on next season, not a peak.

**Working FTP lives in intervals.icu**, in sport settings — not in this file. Every
target here is a percentage of it, so the number exists in exactly one place and
moves in exactly one way (see *Re-anchoring FTP*).

**LTHR 170 bpm**, measured over the last 20 min of a 52-min max effort in Jan 2026.
Also set in intervals.icu.

---

## How to use this document

This is the whole brief. Read it before prescribing anything.

1. **Prescribe in percentages, never watts.** A watt number written into a plan goes
   stale silently — that is the documented root cause of the 2026 plateau. The only
   exceptions are the Wednesday sprints and a leaderboard attempt, both of which say
   so where they appear.
2. **Every quality session is a rung on a ladder.** See *The ladders*. Don't invent a
   session shape; take the next rung.
3. **Two quality sessions a week. Not three.**
4. **Keep the Thursday/Sunday swap intact.** Every week must work in both variants.
5. **Explain the physiology of each session.** I'm learning to self-coach. A session I
   can't justify is a session I'll drop in November.
6. **Flag every assumption. Don't invent data.**
7. **Don't hedge.** Commit to a prescription and defend it.

**What I'll give you:** how sessions felt, and whether a group ride is on the
calendar. **Pull the rest yourself** — per-rep power and HR, CTL/ATL, HRV and RHR are
all in the store. Do ask about RPE, which no API carries — but treat it as ±1.

**What I want back:** the week day by day, with durations, `%FTP` targets, recoveries,
expected HR, and one line on what each session is for. Both variants if a group ride
is uncertain.

## The athlete

- 27M, 72 kg, 5'10". Structured training since Oct 2025 — **this is year 2**.
- **8–12 h/week, built around 10–11.** 5–6 days. Longest ride 4–5 h on one weekend day.
  This is a commitment, not a description of last year — see *Assumptions*.
- **Weekdays indoor** (smart trainer), **weekends outdoor**. Same power meter both.
- NYC, rolling terrain. Sleep 8 h, low stress, weight stable and staying there —
  **this is a watts goal, not a weight goal.**

**Constraints, in priority order:**

1. **Hard group rides with friends are a sub-goal, not a cost.** About one a month,
   occasionally two, often unplanned. Keeping them may delay 288 W; I'd rather delay it than cut them.
2. **Burnout is the main risk.** Given a choice between more load and more
   sustainability, choose sustainability.
3. **Gym is a second goal, not a support act.** At least one session a week. It won't
   add watts by December and isn't prescribed to. See *Strength*.
4. **No trash miles.** Easy rides must be genuinely easy.
5. **Sharpening can wait.** Almost no VO2 work through December is deliberate.

## Why this plan looks like this

FTP has been flat at 265–275 W since January. Three causes, and the plan is built
around them. Full evidence is in [`plan/decisions.md`](plan/decisions.md).

**The limiter is fractional utilization, not top end.** FTP ÷ 4-min max is 73.5%;
trained cyclists sit at 75–85%. That's a normal ceiling on an underbuilt engine, so
the target is already inside my existing ceiling. **Consequence: threshold and
durability work, almost no VO2, through December.**

**I never re-anchored.** FTP was set in January and trained off through June, so every
session slid down into maintenance while I kept doing it. Hence: percentages against
one number, retested every block.

**I progressed reps, never watts — and barely progressed reps.** Between 24 Jan and
14 May I climbed exactly two rungs of the sweet spot ladder (2×20 → 3×15 → 2×25) and
finished feeling good. A finished progression should end at the edge. **That is what
the ladders are for.**

**Thesis: harder sessions, not more of them, and much easier easy days.**

## Targets

Four numbers. Nothing else has a name, and no session is described any other way.

| | % FTP | % LTHR | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Easy / Z2** | **60–75%**, ride NP ≤72% | 78–85% | everything that isn't a quality session |
| **Tempo** | **85%** | 84–88% | durability blocks late in long rides |
| **Sweet spot** | **90%** | 87–91% | the quality sessions of Block 1 |
| **Threshold** | **97%** | 93–99% | from Block 2 on |

**Hold the number, ±2%.** Indoors in erg that's exact; outdoors ±2% is the band.
Recovery is anything under 60% and means nothing at all.

**Short efforts are prescribed by their own protocol** and carry no band expectation —
warm-up primers, openers, over-unders, sprints. **Sprints are the one absolute target
in the plan:** neuromuscular, roughly 3× FTP, written in watts, and they do not move
when FTP moves.

### Reading HR

- **Judge a rep on its last 5 minutes**, not its average.
- HR stays roughly **flat across a tempo or sweet spot session**. That's what
  "repeats weekly without digging a hole" looks like — not a sign the target is soft.
- HR **climbs across a threshold session** rather than sitting at LTHR from rep 1.
- **Expect higher HR at the same %FTP in heat**, and after 2+ hours. That's the cost
  being paid, not a signal.
- **HR won't rise despite maximal RPE: stop.** See the hard stops.

## The ladders

**Every quality session is one rung.** Adapted from Tim Cusick, *Building Fatigue
Resistance* (WKO4), archived in [`plan/sources/`](plan/sources/wko4-fatigue-resistance.md).
Recovery between reps is **5 min at 55%**, except threshold, which gets **8 min**.

| # | Tempo @ 85% | Sweet spot @ 90% | Threshold @ 97% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1×30 | 1×15 | 3×10 |
| 2 | 2×20 | 2×20 | 3×15 |
| 3 | 1×45 | 3×15 | 5×10 |
| 4 | 2×30 | 2×25 | 2×25 |
| 5 | 1×60 | 1×50 | 6×10 |
| 6 | 2×45 | 4×15 | 3×20 |
| 7 | 1×90 | 3×20 | 1×60 |
| 8 | | 2×30 | |
| 9 | | 1×60 | |

**The rules:**

1. **One rung per quality session.** Two quality sessions a week, so about two rungs a
   week. A week with one quality session advances one rung.
2. **A rung only counts if it was completed at target** — every rep at the number ±2%,
   the last rep intact. Otherwise it doesn't count and you ride it again. This is the
   whole point: it stops the ladder drifting away from what was actually ridden.
3. **One repeat, normally.** Repeating a rung twice means something is wrong — look at
   fuelling, sleep, and the check-in before looking at the target.
4. **The repeat is the back-off.** Repeating is not failure, it is the cheapest
   intervention available, and there is no limit on it when the check-in or the
   markers say back off. A recovery week has **no rung at all** — not a smaller one.
5. **Never skip a rung**, and never jump ladders mid-block.
6. **You don't have to finish a ladder.** Blocks end at the test, not at the last rung.
7. **Watts move only at the test.** Within a block the rung moves. Between blocks the
   FTP moves and the same rung is worth more. Never both in the same week.

**Why they're shaped like this.** Each ladder alternates the axis it stresses — one
step adds reps, the next adds length — so you never raise total volume and continuous
duration together. And each ladder goes **flat in time-in-zone near the top**: sweet
spot rungs 6–9 are all 60 min in band, threshold rungs 5–7 all 60. Past that point the
only thing progressing is fragmentation, and Cusick is explicit that this is a
**central and psychological** adaptation rather than a physiological one — there is no
metabolic difference between 4×15 and 1×60, and that is exactly why 1×60 is harder.

**The Sunday durability blocks are not rungs.** They're depleted, outdoors, and capped
by the length of the ride. They use the **tempo target** and progress on their own slow
track. Don't count them, and don't let them set the ladder.

## The standard warm-up

Before every quality session. **22 minutes.**

1. 8 min ramping 48% → 70%
2. **3 × 1 min @ 100–105%**, 2 min @ 52% between — firm, not maximal
3. 5 min @ 55%

**Why:** oxygen uptake kinetics are sluggish at session start, so rep 1 runs
disproportionately anaerobic. Prior hard work speeds VO2 kinetics on later efforts, so
pay that priming cost in the warm-up where it's cheap.

**Start rep 1 at the target.** No ramp-in, no easy first two minutes. The primers are
what solve the first-rep problem; a soft opening just makes the rep shorter than it
says it is.

**Diagnostic:** rep 1 hard and reps 2–3 fine is a warm-up problem, and a normal one.
Don't change targets over it.

## The week

**Tuesday is always on. Thursday and a hard Sunday are the swappable pair.**

### Variant A — Thursday kept, Sunday is Z2

| Day | Session | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Off, or 40 min recovery | Nothing. Genuinely nothing. |
| **Tue** | **The next rung.** Warm-up → rung → 10 min CD. **Gym after** | The anchor. It never moves. |
| **Wed** | 70 min easy + **4 × 15 s standing starts, MAX**, full recovery between | Aerobic volume. Sprints cost nothing and keep neuromuscular recruitment alive. |
| **Thu** | **The rung after that.** Same shape | The droppable session, so it's the one whose absence costs least. |
| **Fri** | 60 min easy, or off. **Light gym slot** (upper/core, no legs) | |
| **Sat** | Outdoor endurance 2–2.5 h | Volume. |
| **Sun** | Outdoor 3–3.5 h Z2. **Final 45 min: durability blocks at tempo** | Tempo *after* 2.5 h trains fractional utilization directly. It costs more than the same watts fresh; that's the point. |

**~10.5 h.**

> **On the Wednesday sprints.** Actual standing starts: out of the saddle, big gear,
> surge as hard as you can, hold form, let it fade across the 15 s. Measured efforts
> run 550–920 W. The **500–800 W** on the workout is a deliberately wide range so no
> head unit treats it as a number to hold — the instruction is *max*. Full recovery
> between (4+ min); if the 4th is materially down on the 1st, stop.

### Variant B — Thursday dropped, Sunday is a hard group ride

Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday unchanged. **Thursday becomes 75 min easy + 5 × 1 min @
95–102%** — openers, not a workout. **Saturday is 90 min–2 h easy only**; do not
pre-fatigue Sunday. **Sunday is the group ride, and it is the second quality effort,
not a compromise.** **Monday after is a full day off.** ~9.5–10 h.

**The ladder advances one rung that week, on Tuesday.** A group ride is not a rung.

### Choosing between them

**Default is keep Thursday. Cutoff is Wednesday 21:00.** These rides happen 1–2× a
month, so Variant A runs three weeks out of four.

- Drop Thursday **only** if a hard Sunday is confirmed or ≥60% likely.
- **Genuinely 50/50:** ride Thursday's rung anyway. If Sunday goes hard you've paid a
  little extra fatigue; if it stays Z2 you've banked a rung.
- **Group ride lands Saturday:** Saturday is the hard day, Sunday is 2–3 h pure Z2.
- **Group ride appears Thursday night:** ride it, skip the rung, make Sunday pure Z2.
- **Both happen anyway:** three quality efforts, survivable about once a month. Next
  week take no rung on Thursday and make it easy. Pay the debt immediately.

**A group ride was a hard day if IF ≥ 0.80 or TSS ≥ 220.** Score it afterward. If it
was, the following Monday is off and that week gets one quality session.

## Strength

**A secondary goal in its own right** — muscle mass, testosterone, bone density,
connective-tissue resilience, and a base for real lifting in the off-season. It is not
prescribed as a route to 288 W and won't add watts by December.

**At least one session a week, non-negotiable at that floor.** The 45 min sits outside
the riding budget rather than inside it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Heavy day — Tuesday**, after the ride | The only slot for loaded legs. Soreness lands on Wednesday's easy ride and has cleared by Thursday. |
| **Light day — Friday or Saturday** | Upper body, core, mobility. Fine here *because it isn't loaded legs*. |

**Loaded legs are Tuesday-only.** Never the day before a hard group ride, never within
48 h of a test. If Tuesday doesn't happen, move it to Friday or Saturday and drop it to
the light template — a light session that happens beats a heavy one that doesn't.

**Through December, submaximal: 2–3 reps in reserve.** Revisit at each block boundary.
Loads resolve to kilograms at push time from anchors in `plan/decisions.md`; keep the
prescription relative here. **Ramping in** is the exception and it's short — unaccustomed
eccentric loading causes soreness peaking at 24–48 h that fades fast with exposure, so
the whole cost is in the first few weeks. **Don't add load and volume in the same
week** — the same rule the bike runs on.

**If 288 W is on a knife edge in November, the gym is a lever worth naming:** cut the
light session, or drop the heavy session's legs, before touching the bike. The failure
mode to watch is a gym session quietly becoming a third quality day. **Flag missed
sessions in the weekly review.**

## Heat

Multiply the target by the factor. Applies to **tempo and above**.

| Dew point | Factor |
|---|---|
| <60°F | ×1.00 |
| 60–65°F | ×0.98 |
| 65–70°F | ×0.96 |
| 70–75°F | ×0.94 |
| >75°F | ×0.92, or move indoors |

A further ×0.98 above 88°F air temp. Indoors with two fans and a room under 75°F: no
adjustment. Indoors with poor airflow: treat as 70–75°F dew point.

**The derate is not a reduction in training stress** — the adjusted wattage costs what
the unadjusted one costs indoors. You pay the difference in thermal strain. Don't apply
Z2 HR expectations to a summer outdoor ride at all; cap those by power.

**Quality work defaults indoors** in this weather. It's a default, not a rule: **when I
ask for a quality session outdoors, it goes outdoors** — apply the derate and prescribe
it properly. Two things genuinely argue for indoors: reps longer than ~10 min need road
that won't chop them up, and a rung being compared against the ones either side of it
is cleaner in matched conditions.

### Reading power that looks low

| HR | RPE | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | Normal for the power | Heat or dehydration | Not a fitness signal. Drop to the adjusted target and finish. |
| Normal | **High** | Glycogen depletion or accumulated fatigue | Cut the set one rep short. Eat more. |
| **Low** despite genuine max effort | Very high | Deep fatigue, overreaching, or illness | **Stop.** See the hard stops. |

## Fuelling

The Sunday session works *because* it lands on depleted glycogen — but that state comes
from the duration in front of it, not from under-eating.

| Ride | Carbohydrate |
|---|---|
| Easy, under ~90 min | Nothing needed |
| Quality session, 60–90 min | 40–60 g/h — protects the last reps |
| 90 min – 2.5 h | ~60 g/h |
| 3–4 h | **80–90 g/h.** Needs glucose+fructose; single-source absorption tops out near 60 g/h |
| After anything hard or long | ~80–90 g carb + ~25 g protein within the hour |

**No caloric deficit during build blocks.** Multi-day trips are where under-fuelling
compounds.

## Re-anchoring FTP

**Test at the end of every block.** Take intervals.icu's eFTP off the test — it
computes it from the power-duration curve and there's no need to recompute by hand.
Then:

1. Update **`ftp` and `indoor_ftp` together** in sport settings
   (`IntervalsClient.update_sport_settings`, cycling id `953869`). If they diverge, the
   same `%` workout means different watts indoors and out, and the CTL series quietly
   mixes both.
2. Log the change and its evidence in [`plan/decisions.md`](plan/decisions.md).

**Between tests, the working FTP does not move.** Not off a session that felt easy, not
off a high eFTP reading on one ride. intervals.icu does not auto-update FTP from eFTP,
so the number only ever changes when I change it — keep it that way.

**If a test comes in below the current FTP:** lower it, unless the test was hot, run in
a fatigued week, or post-illness — in which case retest within 10 days rather than
carrying a number neither of us believes.

Testing every 4–5 weeks is what makes progressing rungs inside a block safe. The 2026
failure was that pattern running for seven months unbounded.

## Load and backing off

**The default is to progress.** Most weeks, take the next rung.

**Prefer a recovery week every 4th week.** Push to a 5th when the calendar demands it.
Don't run six build weeks without one.

**When to cut load or pull the recovery week forward:** some combination of motivation
dropping off, HRV trending down against its own baseline, and RHR drifting up. **Two of
the three moving together** is worth acting on; any one alone is noise. This is a
judgement call made in the weekly review, and **repeating a rung is the cheapest
available intervention.**

**Two hard stops, which are not judgement calls:**

1. **HR won't rise during intervals at maximal RPE.** Stop, several days easy minimum.
   That's the classic parasympathetic overreaching signature and the one most amateurs
   push through.
2. **Sore throat, swollen glands, or fever.** Stop entirely.

**One question, every week, whenever it gets asked:**

> *If I deleted this week's hard sessions right now — disappointed, neutral, or
> relieved?*

"Relieved" two weeks running means cut to one quality session, whatever the numbers
say. The physiological markers detect accumulated fatigue; none of them can detect a
fading *reason* to train, and that is what actually ended the last block. Log answers
in [`plan/check-ins.md`](plan/check-ins.md).

Read the trend, not the week. Ask it whenever in the week it fits.

**Any week with three quality efforts is followed by a week with one.**

## Assumptions

**Confirmed:** indoor and outdoor power meters are the same unit. LTHR 170, possibly
1–3 bpm generous. Max HR ~194 working figure, and nothing here is prescribed off it.
HRV and RHR are in intervals.icu nightly, with SD ~14 — read a trend, never a single
morning. Per-rep watts and HR are readable from the store without asking. RPE is the
one thing no API carries, and it resolves to ±1.

**Still assumed:**

1. The January 52-min effort was a genuine max. Everything anchors there.
2. **A week off after 4 July, then two weeks ill** — the weeks of 13 and 20 Jul came in
   at 1.0 h and 2.8 h of riding against 7–10 h either side. Some light riding and a run,
   but a genuine three-week interruption. Assumed to have cost ~5–8% at threshold,
   largely recoverable in 3–4 weeks. Ordinary respiratory illness, fully resolved.
3. The August 18-min effort was hot and came two weeks off the couch — intervals read
   it as 265 W eFTP, so true FTP is plausibly higher than 270. The block test settles it.
4. **The plan needs ~10.5 h/week, and that is its largest single assumption.** Measured
   weekly hours Jan–May 2026 ran 7–8.5 in a typical build week, with five weeks out of
   twenty-two above 10 h — the best stretch being 10.5, 9.9, 11.3 across late Apr and
   May. So 10–11 sustained sits at the top of the demonstrated range rather than inside
   it. **Forward test: four consecutive weeks above 10 h by the end of Block 1.** If
   hours settle nearer 7.5, 288 W by December doesn't arrive, and what needs re-cutting
   is the roadmap, not the week.
5. Weight stays 72 kg with no restriction during build blocks.
6. **One gym session a week costs the bike little enough to ignore**, and strength adds
   **no watts by December** — the target is built without it, so a pleasant surprise
   changes nothing and the absence of one costs nothing.
